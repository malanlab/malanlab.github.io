## 🧠 Which cortical oscillatory features and brain regions are associated with chronic pain severity?

<div class="card">

### Problem

Chronic pain is associated with abnormal cortical rhythms, particularly in the alpha band (8–12 Hz). However, it remains unclear how peak alpha frequency (PAF) and alpha power change across brain regions, and how these changes relate to subjective pain severity.

</div>

<div class="card">

### What we studied

We used resting-state magnetoencephalography (MEG) in patients with Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS) and healthy controls to estimate:

- Peak alpha frequency (PAF)
- Alpha-band power across cortex
- Functional connectivity in alpha networks
- Relationship with pain severity

</div>

<div class="card">

### Key findings

- CRPS shows **global slowing of peak alpha frequency (PAF)**
- **Reduced alpha power**, especially in:
  - Precuneus  
  - Paracentral cortex  
  - Superior parietal cortex  

- Pain severity is linked to:
  - Reduced PAF power in **prefrontal cortex (PFC)** and **orbitofrontal cortex (OFC)**
  - Increased alpha-band connectivity in PFC–OFC networks

</div>

<div class="card">

### Interpretation

Alpha oscillations reflect both sensory and affective dimensions of chronic pain:

- Posteromedial regions encode **sensory-discriminative aspects**
- Prefrontal and orbitofrontal regions encode **subjective pain intensity**
- Network hyperconnectivity suggests maladaptive integration of pain-related affect

</div>

</div>

## ⚡ How does the human cerebellum interact with cortex during motor error processing?

<div class="card">

### Problem

The cerebellum is essential for motor learning and error correction, but how cerebello–cortical networks interact during real-time motor error processing in humans remains unclear.

</div>

<div class="card">

### What we study

We investigate cerebello–cortical communication during motor tasks using:

- EEG/MEG recordings
- Behavioral error paradigms
- Connectivity and oscillatory coupling analysis
- Computational modeling of error correction

</div>

<div class="card">

### Key questions

- How does the cerebellum communicate error signals to cortex?
- Which cortical regions encode motor error updates?
- How does this interaction change during learning and adaptation?

</div>

<div class="card">

### Interpretation

We aim to uncover how distributed cerebello–cortical loops enable:

- Error detection
- Adaptive motor correction
- Trial-to-trial learning in humans

</div>

</div>

## 🚶 How does cortico–subcortical network dysfunction lead to freezing of gait in Parkinson’s disease?

<div class="card">

### Problem

Freezing of gait (FOG) is a disabling symptom of Parkinson’s disease, but its neural basis is still not fully understood. In particular, how cortico–subcortical network breakdown leads to sudden motor block remains unclear.

</div>

<div class="card">

### What we study

We analyze patients with Parkinson’s disease using:

- EEG and Local Field Potentials (LFP)
- Deep brain stimulation (DBS) recordings
- Motor task paradigms
- Beta-band dynamics and burst activity
- Functional connectivity and network analysis

</div>

<div class="card">

### Key hypotheses

- FOG arises from **disrupted cortico–basal ganglia communication**
- Abnormal **beta bursts** reflect impaired motor state switching
- DBS modulates these networks and restores motor flow

</div>

<div class="card">

### Interpretation

We aim to explain FOG as a **network-level failure of motor state transitions**, linking cortical control, basal ganglia dynamics, and therapeutic DBS effects.

</div>
